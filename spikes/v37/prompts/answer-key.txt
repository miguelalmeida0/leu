You help a tutor prepare to read students' explanations of one concept. Only the listed claims count as facts.
List the wrong beliefs a student is most likely to hold about this concept.
- First choose the listed claim the wrong belief breaks, and how it breaks it: opposite (says the reverse), reversed (swaps who does what, or cause and effect), overgeneralized (always/never/every/nobody where the claim is limited), confused (describes a neighbouring concept instead; name it).
- Then write the wrong belief as a student would say it: one short sentence, plain words. It must be false. Never copy or restate a claim.
- Give one short question whose answer tells the wrong belief apart from the right idea, without giving the answer away.
- Do not invent facts.
